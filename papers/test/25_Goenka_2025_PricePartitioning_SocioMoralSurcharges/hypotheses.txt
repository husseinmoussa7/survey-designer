H1: Partitioned (vs. all-inclusive) pricing for socio-moral surcharges that aim to raise funds for social causes leads to negative consumer reactions. (Study 1A & Study 1B & Study 2)

H2: The effect of partitioned pricing on consumer reactions will be driven by avoidance of responsibility perceptions and CSR attributions in sequence.(Study 2)

H3: The negative consumer reactions toward partitioned (vs. all-inclusive) pricing for socio-moral surcharges will be attenuated when firms signal that the funds are being directed to an external party and will not be retained by them. (Study 4)

H4: The negative consumer reactions toward partitioned (vs. all-inclusive) pricing for socio-moral surcharges will be attenuated when firms credibly signal social actions that they take to support the cause. (Study 5)

H5: The negative consumer reactions toward partitioned (vs. all-inclusive) pricing for socio-moral surcharges will be attenuated when the surcharge is framed as a benefit for consumers. (Study 6)
